Most crawl space quotes are guesses until someone actually goes under the house. Moisture, drainage, framing, insulation, and access all vary from home to home, and the right scope depends on what the inspection finds, not on a standard package.

Catawba Crawlspace Co. inspects the crawl space and documents conditions with photos so the homeowner can see each issue: exposed soil, standing water, damp insulation, wood staining, vent condition, and drainage paths.

Moisture and Humidity Check

Catawba Crawlspace Co. checks exposed soil, humidity, condensation, and damp materials to gauge the crawl space moisture load.

Drainage and Water Review

Standing water, grading, downspout discharge, and foundation seepage are reviewed to find where bulk water enters.

Structural Look

Floor joists, beams, piers, sill plates, and subfloor are checked for wood rot, damage, and moisture staining.

Insulation and Vents

Existing insulation and foundation vents are assessed for condition and fit with the recommended approach.

Is the crawl space inspection really free?

Yes. Catawba Crawlspace Co. inspects and photo-documents the crawl space and delivers a clear scope at no cost, so you can make a decision based on the actual conditions before spending anything.

What does the inspection cover?

Moisture and humidity, drainage and standing water, framing and supports, insulation, vents, and access. The findings are photographed so you can see the crawl space without going under the house.

Do I have to be home for the inspection?

It helps so the crew can walk you through the findings, but arrangements can vary. The important part is that you receive photo-documented results and a plain-language scope afterward.

Will I be pressured to buy a full encapsulation?

No. The scope reflects what the crawl space actually needs. If it only needs part of a system, that is what is recommended, and there is no obligation to move forward immediately.

How long does a crawl space inspection take?

Most inspections take under an hour depending on crawl space size and access. The goal is a thorough, documented look that supports a confident decision.

What do I get at the end?

A photo-documented picture of the crawl space and a written, plain-language scope and estimate: what is wrong, what it takes to fix, and what can wait.
